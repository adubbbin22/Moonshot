# Round 2: Fintech/brokerage expert

## Endorse / challenge / merge

- **Endorse:** resolution spec plus horizon guard. A bet that expires before its question resolves is a misleading communication, so this is a compliance control too.
- **Merge:** Call → Reveal → Express with the probability slider. The reveal is the most recommendation-like moment in the product. It stays compliant if:
  - every user on a side sees the same bets
  - there's no sizing and no ranking by fit
  - the bets are labelled "ways to express this view"
  - "no bet" shows when your number is at or below the market's

  Drop the word "edge": say "You vs. market: +24 pts."
- **Endorse:** the one-glance bet screen. Above the fold: "You can lose all $116" next to the multiple, and "market-implied, not a forecast". The multiple uses executable prices.
- **Merge:** Thesis Tree + the conditional tree + the compiler. Challenge: an LLM writing "tanker owners are the less crowded way" is an unsubstantiated investment opinion. That belongs in human-reviewed catalog copy, identical for every user.
- **Challenge receipts:** a realized multiple *is* a return. Show the multiple and odds at entry only, and the call outcome at resolution.
- **Challenge Fund I:** "Fund" and "LP" imply a pooled vehicle. Use "Vintage 2027". The supernova fires when the *call* resolves right, never on dollars.
- **Defer:** thesis authors. That's a finfluencer program, so leave it until after product-market fit.
- **Endorse, worded precisely:** "Moonshot earns nothing from your trades". Keep the broker's Rule 606 PFOF disclosures.

## Positions

- **T1:** free forecasting is the daily loop. Money is the paid "express" step: a ~$9/mo membership, with sweep income later. Two rules:
  - No prizes of value. That drifts into sweepstakes and gambling law.
  - The record never pushes betting. "You've shown edge, now bet" is a gamified unlock.
- **T2:** there is a compliant version.
  - *The LLM may:* restate the belief as a claim that can resolve; propose steps from a curated mechanism library; name sectors in general terms; steelman the other side; draft kill criteria for the user to accept; explain structures; summarize sourced news.
  - *Code must own:* the mapping from mechanism to catalog bet; every number, including the product of the user's own conditionals; ordering, sizing and the "no bet" rule.
  - *Pointers:* the LLM references a bet only by a pointer that code renders.
  - *If registered as an adviser:* the LLM may tailor the shortlist and sizing to the user's assets under a best-interest duty, with advice records kept. Numbers still come from code.
- **T3:** no event contracts at launch. Later, a separate account through Alpaca's FCM: economic and financial contracts only, with their own disclosures and likely NFA registration as an introducing broker. No sports, elections or war, and no marketing of longshots.
- **T4:**
  - Narrow spreads: mid debit ÷ width (model-free).
  - Long options: derive odds from a tight vertical spread on the chain.
  - Multiple and 3x check: natural (executable) price.
  - Scoring: against mid-implied odds at call time, with a published, fixed tail-bias haircut, never shown as "true odds".
- **T5:** opt-in by theme and archived.
  - *Allowed:* service messages about your own positions (kill-criterion signals with sources, the close-out window, assignment or ex-dividend risk); resolutions of your calls; a weekly digest of questions only.
  - *Never:* price-move pushes, unheld tickers, "trade now", streak guilt, or bet suggestions after a loss.
- **T6:** tiers of $250, $500 and $1,000; drop $100. Default $500 with the 30% cap.
  - The catalog is filtered by cap, approval level and liquidity, and says so ("At $250/mo, 6 of 17 fit").
  - Target $40–150 per contract.
  - Never upsell a bigger budget, and never coach answers on the options application.
- **T7:** Barbell, pending trademark. "Moonshots" becomes the name of the sleeve. "Keep your index fund" avoids a comparative claim.
- **T8:** merge. Launch "Beat the market's odds" (no license needed, a weekly loop, the story for regulators), presented as night-sky Vintages. The Barbell Account, as a registered adviser, is the mature money product that makes LLM personalization legal. Keep the "Crowd vs. Wall Street" board at the event level.
- **T9:**
  - *Safe:* calibration seasons and leagues with no budget resets or bet deadlines; check-in streaks with no loss-aversion pushes; "Honest exit", as long as it isn't counted.
  - *Unsafe:* "Early" on bets, because it rewards buying longshots. Make it forecast-only and gate it on calibration.

## Final top 5

1. **Resolvable questions plus horizon guard.** Metric: dispute rate under 1%.
2. **Call → Reveal, calibration record and Called-it cards.** Metric: D30 retention among users with a resolved call, 25%+.
3. **One-glance bet screen on executable prices.** Metric: 90%+ of buyers can answer "Most you can lose? What has to happen?"
4. **Thesis Tree as a firewalled compiler.** Metric: zero firewall violations in sampled review.
5. **Lifecycle autopilot plus Thesis Health.** Metric: unplanned assignments per 1,000 positions near zero.

Guardrail across all five: engagement rises while trades per user stay flat.

## What changed

- **Forecasting-first is the strongest compliance posture available,** not just a retention tactic.
- **The horizon mismatch is a disclosure defect** that the integrity test should catch.
- **Gamification must not subsidize the longshot bias.**
